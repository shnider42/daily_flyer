 THE QUICK HUDDLE

Football. Thumb-sized.

Ways to play: Exhibition offers 7 vs 7 or 11 vs 11 with a wider field view, five linemen and a lead blocker. Training has three stages each for passing, running, field goals and punts. Legends offers four playable historic moments with saved stars and instant retries. Classic Games starts those matchups from kickoff using curated real game-day players and arcade ratings. Choose any of the eight throwbacks in Exhibition. Historical scenes are playable interpretations, not exact simulations or archival footage.

Game pace: The play clock allows 40 seconds to call and snap; kicks from scrimmage use 25. Delay of game moves the offense back five yards (half the distance near its goal) without losing a down. Pause, shows and replays hold the clock. TV timeouts are six-second, skippable breaks, at most once per quarter before the fourth. Both options can be changed before a match. Training and Legends use their own attempt flow.

  1. On offense, call a play, then tap SNAP at the bottom right. The blue line is the line of scrimmage. Reach the gold line for a fresh set of four downs.
  2. Run with your left thumb. Drag the stick. BURST gives a short speed boost. Near contact it becomes BREAK TACKLE: steer sideways for a spin, meet a side hit with a stiff-arm, or drive forward to bulldoze. Each attempt costs stamina and can expose the ball. Strength, agility, balance and the tackler’s skills decide the contest; nearby defenders make escape harder. A difficult catch or sharp reversal can leave you off balance. Repeated taps do not add chances.
  3. Pass with A, B or C. Buttons match the letters above receivers. You control the catcher immediately. Passing ends once you cross the blue line.
  4. On defense, choose coverage and get set. The CPU snaps after its cadence. Move before the snap, but stay on your side of the blue line. Tap a defender on the field to select them. NEAREST selects the best available defender near the ball; NEXT cycles the team. Before the snap, PICK opens a named player list. Keys 1, 2 and 3 do the same. Opponent routes and play calls stay hidden. DIVE commits to a tackle; a miss briefly slows you down. If blocked, that button becomes SHED to break free.

Arcade match rules: choose four 45-second (Quick), 90-second (Extended) or 3-minute (Sunday showcase) quarters. The clock runs during plays; touchdowns score six, followed by a playable extra-point kick. Interceptions and defensive fumble recoveries can be returned for touchdowns. Possession flips the field so the ball carrier always runs upscreen. Fourth down offers a playable punt or field goal up to 65 yards (check your kicker’s range). Kickoffs open each half and follow scores. Tied games go to sudden-death overtime.

Field goals and extra points: Tap START METER, tap SET POWER in the green range, then tap KICK with the accuracy marker in the green center. Distance and kicker ratings set the required power and accuracy window; your timing decides the result. Short kicks fall short, off-center kicks go wide. The clock stops during the extra point.

Uniforms: Your home team wears dark jerseys; the away team wears white with team-colored numbers. Uniforms stay with each team through turnovers and replays.

Special teams: Aim with the stick or A/B/C (left/center/right). Tap START METER, then KICK to set power. For incoming kicks, tap READY TO RETURN. The returner tracks the landing ring until you move the stick. On punts, B signals a fair catch. After the catch, run upfield and burst; when kicking, switch and dive to tackle. Missed catches can roll or be downed. Arcade touchbacks: kickoff at the 25, punt at the 20; kickoff out of bounds at the 40.

Formation packages: Singleback, Pistol and Bunch right each offer runs, passes and play-action from the same alignment. Special looks retain the wing, jet and offset calls. The offense commits first; the defense then calls coverage with the personnel already on the field. Base, Nickel and Heavy use different roster players and stay locked for the possession. Change your next defensive unit in Pause.

Play-action: The QB keeps the ball through a brief fake; pass buttons unlock afterward. Linebackers and safeties read the fake using difficulty, recognition, QB/RB ratings and the last 24 snapped calls from that formation. Establishing the run helps; repeated fakes lose credibility. A fooled defender takes a brief step toward the run, then recovers. The defender you control always follows your input.

Blocking: Follow your linemen through the lane. Pass blockers form a pocket; run blockers and receivers take separate threats. Blocks wear out, so release the pass before protection breaks down.

Flags: Crossing the line at the snap is offside (5 yards); pre-snap contact is encroachment (5 yards, dead ball). A beaten pocket blocker can grab and draw holding (10 yards, repeat the down). Near a goal line, penalties use half the distance; holding in your own end zone is a safety. Live flags are settled after the play. When the choice is yours, ACCEPT is at bottom right and DECLINE is above it. The CPU chooses for its team. Offsetting fouls replay the down. This is a limited arcade penalty set; return holding and pass interference are not modeled yet.

Ball security: Catchable passes occasionally drop; defenders can tip passes that stay live until they hit the turf. Contact can cause a fumble, with extra risk on sacks, dives and bursts. Chase the loose ball with the stick; NEAREST and NEXT still work on defense. Either team can recover. Own fourth-down recoveries are dead and cannot advance beyond the fumble spot. End-zone interceptions are automatic touchbacks at the 20.

Difficulty changes decisions: Rookie reacts later and guesses more. Pro reads situations and recent run/pass tendencies. All-pro reacts sooner, covers more precisely and takes better pursuit angles. Player speed and ball-outcome odds stay the same. Each team has 32–33 offensive calls and 12 defensive calls, with signature plays. Guidance changes explanations and suggestions, independently of difficulty.

Special teams: Choose your kicker, punter, kick and punt returners, and two gunners in the locker room. Before receiving a kick, choose Return Left, Middle or Right with the three buttons (1–3 on desktop); that sets the early blocking lane. You still steer the returner. Punt fair catches remain available in flight. The box score tracks kicking, punting, coverage and return production separately from interception returns.

Instant replay: Touchdowns, fumbles, interceptions and big special-teams returns are recorded. Made field goals and punts pinned inside the 20 can join the broadcast highlight reel. Replays begin after the play ends, so returns stay live. Tap SKIP REPLAY or press Space to continue. Pause can disable automatic replays. Scores and random outcomes never change during a replay.

Halftime: Every game length includes skippable pregame and halftime shows with original, captioned fan-tribute banter from a pixel-art Al Michaels and John Madden booth. Pregame previews your selected lineups and special-teams unit. Casey Reed, PGI’s fictional field reporter, occasionally checks in from the sideline. Halftime reviews actual first-half highlights with an animated yellow telestrator, plus stats and a little football silliness. Use RUN IT AGAIN to replay a segment, YELLOW MARKER to show or hide the drawings, and PLAY / PAUSE SHOW to control the pace. Illustrations are labeled separately from recorded plays. Optional 8-bit booth chatter follows the highlighted words, with distinct nonverbal sounds for each host. It is on by default, has its own switch in the booth and Pause, and follows the main volume. There are no recorded or impersonated voices. Play freezes during the show; start the second half whenever you are ready.

Stadium soundtrack: Open the soundtrack from the team screen or Pause to choose a song or rotation. Team scoring songs have their own switch; effects and music remain separate.

Desktop: WASD / arrows to move. Space to snap, burst, dive or operate the kick meter. 1–3 pass or aim a kick; on defense 1 selects nearest, 2 cycles, 3 opens the pre-snap picker; 2 calls a fair catch when receiving a punt. P pauses. Switching tabs pauses automatically.

Make the ratings count.

11 vs 11 and animation start on. Endurance governs sustained running: after two seconds carrying the ball costs extra energy, so save burst for a real opening. Defenders also tire, more slowly. Speed separates top-end pace, acceleration separates the first steps, and agility and balance decide cuts and contact.

Injuries are rare simulated match events, limited to two per game and at least 18 snaps apart. A player rests 2–4 snaps; a healthy teammate substitutes automatically. Durability affects the chance. These are game ratings, not real health reports. See Pause for the active list. Training and Legends do not use injuries.

All-Decade teams are curated PGI selections with individual arcade ratings and era-inspired books. The 2020s team is a selection so far. Choose All teams for cross-era games. The 1980s includes Bo Jackson, Walter Payton and Eric Dickerson; change the starter in the locker room.

The halftime coach is a fictional team representative. The interview responds to your match. TV commercials are fictional and always skippable.

Place your passes.

Tap A, B or C while holding the left stick to lead that receiver in the same direction. Up goes farther downfield; down pulls the ball back; left, right and diagonals place it to either shoulder. A small tilt makes a small adjustment. Center or release the stick for assisted placement. The stick still moves your quarterback before the throw. On desktop, hold arrows or WASD while pressing 1, 2 or 3.

Rings preview the placement before QB accuracy and pressure. Receivers adjust on foot; they cannot rescue every throw. A hard upfield lead over a settled hook can sail past it, and a defender under that ball can intercept. Sideline misses go out of bounds. Try a small backward or outside lead for back-shoulder catches.

Each team has 32–33 offensive and 12 defensive calls, including sweeps in both directions, left/right tosses, and a jet sweep. Filter the full book by run, quick or deep passes; or zone, man or pressure. Simple guidance still offers three suggestions.
