Read the rules, coverage & sources

Who counts?

A quarterback enters the cohort after starting at least 12 regular-season games for a single team in any covered season from 1970 onward. Their clock begins with their first such season in the available career history, even before 1970. A combined total of 12 across teams does not qualify without a 12-start team split.

What is year two?

The next calendar season after that first qualifying year, regardless of starts. It is not necessarily the player's second NFL season or next season with 12 starts. Missing years remain gaps. Team changes never reset the clock.

How to read the charts

Career performance uses starter year on X and the selected metric on Y. “Years by quarterback” uses names on X and years since the first qualifying season on Y; each quarterback has a vertical career line. Year 1 is the qualifying season. Hollow points indicate fewer than 12 starts; diamonds mark multi-team totals. Team colors change halfway between annual points when teams change. A gray diamond is a season aggregate, not a fictional team.

Compare eras with care

“Passing efficiency vs. league” subtracts that season's aggregate adjusted net yards per attempt (ANY/A) from the quarterback's ANY/A. Zero is the same-season league baseline. This is calculated here from the source rows and is not PFR's ANY/A+ index. ANY/A accounts for passing yards, touchdowns, interceptions, sacks and sack yards, but not rushing.

Other metrics are not era-adjusted. Schedule length varies; the fixed 12-start threshold is retained, including shortened seasons. The optional year-two threshold selects survivors and changes the research question.

Coverage & limitations

    Source trail

      About graph scales & normalization

      Linear spacing shows equal differences. Logarithmic spacing shows equal ratios and requires positive values. When zero or negative values are present, a requested log scale uses signed log instead, with a visible notice. Signed log uses sign(y) × ln(1 + |y|), is approximately linear near zero and compresses extremes. Tick labels always show values in the displayed units.

      Change from year one subtracts that quarterback's first qualifying season, in the selected metric's units. An unavailable or uncertain year-one baseline produces no normalized line. Career z-scores subtract each quarterback's mean and divide by their population standard deviation, using all available seasons from the first qualifying season onward in this snapshot, not just the visible window. At least two values and nonzero variation are required. Zero is that quarterback's mean; +1 is one standard deviation above it. This compares personal patterns, not absolute ability or era-adjusted rank. Lower remains better for interceptions.

      Separate charts share identical X and Y scales. Custom bounds can clip observations; the count is disclosed above the charts. Missing seasons remain gaps. Normalization, axis scales and spotlight settings do not alter the year-two summary, table or CSV.

      Spread values uses interpolated midranks of all selected, visible observations: crowded values receive more space and distant extremes are compressed. Labels retain actual values, but equal distances no longer mean equal numeric differences; slopes are not rates of change. Ties remain tied. Changing the selection or time window changes this mapping. Zoom middle uses the 10th–90th percentile bounds; out-of-range observations are clipped and counted. Separate charts always share the same mapping.
