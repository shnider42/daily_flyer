Read the rules, coverage & sources

Who counts?

A quarterback enters the cohort after starting at least 12 regular-season games for a single team in any covered season from 1970 onward. Their clock begins with their first such season in the available career history, even before 1970. A combined total of 12 across teams does not qualify without a 12-start team split.

What is year two?

The next calendar season after that first qualifying year, regardless of starts. It is not the player's second NFL season or next season with 12 starts. Missing years remain gaps. Team changes never reset the clock.

How to read the charts

Career performance uses starter year on X and the selected metric on Y. “Years by quarterback” uses names on X and years since the first qualifying season on Y; each quarterback has a vertical career line. Year 1 is the qualifying season. Hollow points indicate fewer than 12 starts; diamonds mark multi-team totals. Team colors change halfway between annual points when teams change. A gray diamond is a season aggregate, not a fictional team.

Compare eras with care

“Passing efficiency vs. league” subtracts that season's aggregate adjusted net yards per attempt (ANY/A) from the quarterback's ANY/A. Zero is the same-season league baseline. This is calculated here from the source rows and is not PFR's ANY/A+ index. ANY/A accounts for passing yards, touchdowns, interceptions, sacks and sack yards, but not rushing.

Other metrics are not era-adjusted. Schedule length varies; the fixed 12-start threshold is retained, including shortened seasons. The optional year-two threshold selects survivors and changes the research question.
