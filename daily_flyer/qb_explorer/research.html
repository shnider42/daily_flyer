02 / THE OUT-OF-SAMPLE TEST

Does knowing year two improve the prediction?

First predict using year one. Then add year two. Test both models on later quarterbacks neither model trained on. If the second model makes smaller errors, year two added information in this test.

What if the same quarterback had a different year two?

Keep year one and the first qualifying season fixed; change year two to see how the estimate responds. This explores the model, not the effect of changing a real player’s performance.

Method, sources & what this cannot tell you

This is an exploratory association study of the bundled PFR-derived snapshot, not a causal study or a validated scouting system. Its completeness limits carry into every result. Film-room filters and selected lines do not affect this independent research cohort.

All included QBs have known, unambiguous year-one and next-calendar-year values. Outcomes start after year two: Hall induction within 25 calendar years; wins as the starting QB in a Super Bowl in the next 10 regular-season years; a recorded 12-start, single-team season in year three; or the unweighted mean league-relative ANY/A in years 3–7, requiring at least three observed values. Incomplete follow-up windows are excluded for everyone—even players whose outcome is already known. Missing year-three observations are excluded, not treated as failures; the year-three and later-efficiency samples therefore have observation/survivor bias.

“No” Hall outcome means not inducted within that window, never “will never be inducted.” A current Hall of Famer can be a negative if inducted later. Super Bowl wins are team outcomes; backup rings and wins in year one/two do not count. Performance and Super Bowl coverage end with the 2024 season; Hall status is as of September 28, 2026.

Pearson r measures linear association (point-biserial for a binary outcome); Spearman uses average ranks for ties. The 95% interval is a 400-resample, QB-level percentile bootstrap with a fixed seed, not adjusted for trying multiple questions. Small groups have large uncertainty. Pattern rates show Wilson 95% intervals; the efficiency outcome shows means with no rate interval. Shared eras, teams, coaching, injuries, starts and opportunities can confound results. A year-one-versus-change correlation is mathematically coupled and can reflect regression to the mean.

The logistic models compare year-one value + first-qualifying calendar year against year-one value + year-two value + that calendar year. Later career stats, Hall labels and wins are never predictor inputs. QBs are sorted by first qualifying season, with the latest approximately 25% held out; a season cohort never crosses the split. Means and standard deviations are fitted only on training data. Models use L2-regularized logistic loss (0.02 penalty, unpenalized intercept), with no tuning on the test set. Brier score is mean squared probability error (lower is better); AUC is pairwise discrimination (0.5 is chance ranking). A prevalence-only baseline is also reported. Labels are reconstructed retrospectively; this cohort holdout is not a point-in-time deployment backtest. One historical split is limited validation, and repeated exploration can overfit the holdout. There is no causal claim, individual probability calibration guarantee, or prediction interval.
