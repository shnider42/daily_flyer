Uncertainty & eligibility

Exploratory association, not causation. Age, role, injuries, team, parks and schedule changes are not controlled. The model includes entry year, but that does not resolve these factors. Testing many statistics increases the chance of finding an accidental pattern.

The rulebook: definitions, coverage & sources

Who is in the study?

Every AL/NL player in the snapshot whose first season with 300 recorded plate appearances (hitters) or 50 innings (pitchers) falls in 1954–2025. A player may have separate hitting and pitching clocks. Pitchers include starters and relievers; hitters can include pitchers with enough plate appearances. Earlier seasons are checked to avoid relabeling established players.

Why 1954? Why these thresholds?

Sacrifice flies, needed for consistent plate appearances and on-base percentage, became a separately recorded statistic in 1954. The thresholds are study conventions, not MLB rookie eligibility or official batting-title qualification. Call-ups and Japanese or minor-league experience do not define year one here.

What stays missing?

Year two is always the next calendar season. No record is not a zero-performance season and a gap is never bridged on the chart. Missing components remain missing. All AL/NL team stints are summed once per player-season; rates are recalculated from totals. Fielding is summed across positions and is not a measure of defensive value.

Playing time changes the story

Totals reflect opportunity as well as performance. Rate statistics from tiny samples are noisy. Strike-shortened seasons, injuries and 2020 remain as recorded; nothing is prorated. Use the substantial-year-two and 2020 filters to compare sensitivity.

What counts as a future?

All outcomes begin after year two. Everyone must have the same completed follow-up window, whether they succeeded or not. No season record means no qualifying AL/NL season for the workload outcomes. Mean future performance requires three measured seasons in years 3–7 and describes survivors, not everyone who began a career. Full observed span is descriptive and remains unfinished for active players.

What can the model claim?

It compares year one + entry year with year one + year two + entry year. Both models use the same earlier training players and later test players. Binary outcomes use regularized logistic regression; future mean performance uses regularized linear regression. All-Star prediction tests any selection, while its chart counts distinct selection seasons. Lower held-out Brier score or squared error means a better result on that particular test set. No model is a guarantee or a causal explanation.
