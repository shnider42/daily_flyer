# Pocket Gridiron: College Classics roster notes

Reviewed 7 November 2026. The player selections, depth charts, attributes, and
playbooks are original PGI curation. These are all-time collections spanning
different eras, not official university all-time teams or historical single-season
depth charts. The three historical challenges have separate actual-season casts
in `college-classic-teams.js`.

## Selection and ratings

Every college collection has a real named roster, a full five-man offensive line,
eleven distinct starters on each side, reserve players, a kicker, a punter, and
return specialists. The seven-player game uses the same athletes. College jersey
numbers are used; a number can repeat when great players from different decades
are combined. No generated filler athletes or copied NFL teams are used.

The three requested quarterbacks are fixed as the initial starters: Doug Flutie
for Boston College, Tim Tebow for Florida, and Tom Brady for Michigan. Ty Law is
Michigan's first cornerback. The depth editor can select another included player.
Brady's attributes describe his Michigan version, rather than importing his
professional career's peak. The same principle applies to every alumnus.

Ratings are authored **college-peak arcade estimates**. They express distinctions
within this collection, not an objective cross-level scale. Tebow and Newton are
powerful runners; Moore wins through accuracy and anticipation; Henry and Jeanty
combine strength with endurance; smaller receivers accelerate and change
direction more quickly. The factory deliberately bypasses the live NFL player
overrides. College and professional team selection remain separate in the lobby.

The broad OL, DL, LB, and DB groups match PGI's compact position system. Historical
ends and flankers map to their receiving or line roles. College positions are
preferred where a famous professional changed positions: Ozzie Newsome appears
with Alabama's receivers, LaMarr Woodley with Michigan's defensive line, Jevon
Kearse with Florida's linebackers, and Winston Venable with Boise's secondary.

## Uniforms and venues

Home and away kits keep each school's helmet identity, pants, jersey colors, and
contrasting numbers. Pixel colors approximate the recognizable traditional look;
they are not reproductions of a particular manufacturer's fabric or every season's
uniform details.

Boise State home exhibitions use blue turf at Albertsons Stadium. Boise's Fiesta
Bowl challenge is at the actual neutral venue and uses green turf. Miami's all-time
home venue deliberately recalls the historical Orange Bowl. The field belongs to
the venue and does not change when possession changes.
