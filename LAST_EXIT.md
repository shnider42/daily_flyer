# LAST EXIT v0.11 — City stories

Boston–Cambridge now has 320 ordinary buildings (previously 200), 18 facade/silhouette types, and towers up to 58 m before rooftop details. New styles include timber triple-deckers, bow-front masonry, glass towers, concrete civic blocks, firehouses, pubs, bookshops and cinemas. Street and river collision layouts remain connected. The original city is an interpretation, not a geographic recreation of Boston.

A camera-oriented panoramic matte adds a warm dawn, Atlantic cloud banks, gulls and an original Boston-inspired skyline: an antenna crown, slanted glass tower, clock tower and dome. River colors, quay details, warm doorsteps, local business signs and a bounded set of falling leaves reinforce the setting. Three rooms use the same real movement and interaction controls with a fixed cutaway camera.

## Solo free-drive additions

- Enter North End Coffee, Common Ground Books and River Works Garage at their marked doorsteps. City map buttons mark the street approach. Walk to the counter and use E or the contextual phone button to talk.
- Rosa, Marek and Dee offer record-parcel, tool-case and coffee-box delivery favors. Carrying is visible on foot. Drive between businesses and hand the item over at the destination counter. There is no timer. The tools favor improves mechanical condition without erasing cosmetic dents.
- One occasional labeled box can appear at a business doorstep. Its gold map marker and local prompt make it discoverable. Picking it up starts a delivery; it cannot duplicate an active favor. Restart resets favors.
- Calm nearby pedestrians give local tips. Hijacked drivers and endangered pedestrians make short procedural gibberish vocalizations, limited by per-speaker and global cooldowns and listener distance. Subtle regional effects include harbor horns, gull-like calls, bells and distant rail noise. Existing sound/effects settings apply; no external audio or speech service is used.
- Interiors are available only in solo free drive, are one room each, and rest the outdoor simulation while occupied. Wanted/reporting states prevent entry. Recovery exits safely. Shared multiplayer retains outdoor driving and the updated environment; room gameplay and favors are not networked.

## Performance and verification

The complete Boston world remains below its existing 45,000-triangle test limit (44,872). Backland buildings use simpler geometry; all static world geometry is batched and culled against the camera before submission. Room furnishing meshes stay below 500 triangles, excluding characters. This limits added rendering work; it does not demonstrate native-device frame rates or solve multiplayer server latency.

170 local checks pass. Additional coverage tests door clearance, room boundaries/counter obstruction, enter/exit/recover state, all three favors and duplicate prevention, boxed deliveries, voice budgets, finite meshes and static culling. Browser QA used the software renderer, including actual desktop entry, walking, a conversation/parcel pickup, and 390×844 / 844×390 phone layouts. Physical iPhone Safari multitouch, audio character and native GPU performance still need device testing.

## Multiplayer work deferred

User observed substantial lag despite successful connection. Before another networking change, measure server tick wall time, event-loop stalls, input acknowledgment age and reconciliation distance under two real devices. Separate client rendering cost from simulation overruns and network latency. Likely next targets are spatial candidate sets for traffic/person/police contacts and avoiding full metadata serialization work per snapshot, then tuning prediction around collision impulses. Changing rates again or buying a larger host without those measurements would hide the cause. This release keeps the existing free service, player cap, tick/snapshot rates and room lifetime.

# LAST EXIT — v0.10

Original DailyFlyer game influenced by Driver 2 on PS1, with street takeovers inspired by later driving games. Boston to Cambridge, Baycrest and Port Meridian, each with three timed jobs, free driving, traffic, police and on-foot exploration. Original procedural assets/audio; no paid services or runtime downloads.

## Drive together (v0.10)

Choose **Drive together → Create room**, then tap the room code to copy an invite link. The second player opens the link and chooses **Drive together → Join room**. Desktop and phone use the same room. This first version supports two drivers in Boston to Cambridge free drive.

- One Node/WebSocket server simulates the city: 96 traffic vehicles, pedestrians, police, vehicle ownership, collisions, deformation, fires, explosions and street objects. Each player starts in a different slow sedan and can get out, walk, run and take a stopped traffic car. A reservation prevents simultaneous takeovers from granting the same car twice.
- Players have separate witness reports and wanted levels. Car/car and car/person impacts, fire and broken props are shared. There are no passengers, cooperative missions, public matchmaking or persistent accounts yet. Solo maps and missions remain available.
- Local movement prediction keeps steering responsive; acknowledged inputs reconcile against the server, other actors interpolate, and nearby entity snapshots arrive at 15 Hz. Local prediction runs at 60 Hz and server player physics at 30 Hz; traffic, civilian decisions and police update at 15 Hz. Distant cars omit detailed state until they return into range. The chase camera rises over close following traffic instead of compressing into the player's car.
- Pausing or losing focus releases the controls and applies the handbrake while the other player continues. A disconnected seat and the original car/damage remain available for 60 seconds. Automatic reconnect uses a per-player secret kept only in sessionStorage; after a reload, Drive together offers Reconnect. Leaving explicitly releases the seat immediately. Server restarts end rooms.
- The server warms its simulation before accepting connections. The prototype allows one active private room on the free server, with bounded inputs, frame sizes, connections, reconnect grace and per-connection backpressure. Inputs carry controls, not client-authored positions, damage or ownership. It is a small prototype, not a competitive anti-cheat system. Internet latency can still cause visible corrections after collisions; physical iPhone Safari, cellular interruption behavior and native GPU performance need device testing.

## Reactive street life (v0.9)

- Civilians react to approaching traffic, near crashes, fire and horns; flee nearby danger, brace at a distant impact, or look toward a horn. Reactions respect building occlusion, expire, and return to the normal walking pace. Their arms and body poses show the reaction; vehicle/debris knockdowns mirror the impact side and recover with a bracing limb.
- Witnesses visibly raise a phone during an active report. Drivers brake and abandon burning or engine-disabled traffic cars once a door is clear. Both exits check walls, terrain, cars and nearby street objects. An existing report follows the driver, junction reservations are released, and the empty car remains with blinking amber hazards and a briefly open door. Cosmetic damage alone does not make drivers abandon a running car. A maximum of 24 temporary escaped drivers is active; blocked/capped exits retry.
- Player horn: H on desktop, or tap the existing speed readout. It uses the same swipe protection and Safari touch guard as the pedals. Obstructed nearby drivers can honk after waiting; normal red-light queues do not. Horns are rate-limited, distance-attenuated and do not create police heat.
- Eleven physical street object types now include dumpsters, wooden crates and striped road barriers. They use the existing impact, blast, tumbling, settling and re-hit systems. A broken hydrant emits a water jet and hiss from its original pipe for 35 seconds; re-hitting the loose hydrant cannot restart the leak. At most four nearby jets render; active debris remains capped at 32.
- Perception updates at roughly 6 Hz, route obstacle probes share that cadence, and gesture/hazard geometry stays lightweight. Portrait and landscape browser views and controlled driver/witness/fire/hydrant scenes were checked. Physical iPhone Safari and native GPU performance remain unverified.

## Dense city foundation (v0.8)

- Boston to Cambridge now has 200 buildings, up from 62, with street frontage that follows bends and additional buildings behind it. Ten types include brownstones, tenements, shops, diners, apartments, offices, warehouses, factories, colleges and a church. Baycrest and Port Meridian now have 180 and 166 ordinary buildings respectively, with six buildings around most blocks, inner courtyards, varied commercial/residential types, and Baycrest gabled row houses. Foundations reach the terrain on slopes. Buildings still do not have enterable interiors.
- 213 physical street objects in Boston, 336 in Baycrest and 328 in Port Meridian: benches, street lamps, bins, hydrants, signs, newspaper boxes, cones and bollards. Placement keeps driving lanes, job stops and sidewalk routes usable.
- Hard impacts release objects into gravity-driven tumbling with angular motion, bounce and friction. Slow bumps stop against anchored heavier objects; light objects can be nudged on foot. Debris settles, stays visible/collidable, and can be struck again. Explosions throw nearby visible furniture. Flying debris can knock a person down. These are lightweight rigid-body approximations, not a full articulated ragdoll solver.
- Broken lamps go dark, bench slats shift, and impacts make brief clatter/sparks. Street damage follows existing witness/report rules. Restart resets street objects; Recover repairs the car without resetting the neighborhood.
- The chase camera also retracts before nearby vehicles, preventing a following van from filling the view.
- Spatial indexes restrict building and furniture collision checks to nearby objects. Distant props use low-detail models; at most 32 objects simulate simultaneously, with older debris settled when the budget is reached.

## Previous map and pursuit update

- Boston to Cambridge is a separate original city with winding embankments, diagonal junctions and unequal blocks. Its connected street graph has 18 junctions and 25 roads. A drivable, white cable-stayed bridge inspired by the Zakim is the only crossing between the two banks. This is not a geographic recreation. The previous two maps remain selectable.
- Six districts: Lantern Hill, North End Yard, Harbor Works, Cambridge Common, College Court and River Foundry. Three new timed jobs cross the river. The title screen retains modest starting cars and remembers your city.
- The new map's traffic follows sampled curved lanes and graph connections, with front-of-queue junction priority. Police also use the real bridge route. Roads, deck, sidewalks, recovery positions and map all share the same layout. River and bridge barriers prevent accidental water shortcuts.
- Boston police wear navy and pale panels; Cambridge police use cream and dark green. Four patrol cars and two uniformed officers are present in the new city. Other cities also have ambient patrols. Dispatch activates units from the incident's side, and their livery persists when a pursuit crosses the bridge.
- Incidents now need an officer's sighting or a civilian report. Vision has distance/facing limits and building/terrain occlusion. Civilian calls take 4.5 seconds, pausing if the witness is stunned; police receive the last observed position, not an unseen player's new coordinates. A knocked-down victim can report after recovering. A heat mission explicitly starts with an existing report. The pickup job no longer creates an automatic pursuit without evidence.
- Severe engine/front or repeated heavy rear impacts can ignite a car, with a warning and roughly 12–14 seconds before explosion. A driver can exit below 18 mph in an emergency. Explosions make visible fire, smoke and a low boom, damage and push nearby cars, and can ignite a delayed chain reaction. Charred wrecks stay in the road and cannot be entered. Cosmetic body damage alone does not ignite cars. A distant unexplained explosion does not identify an unseen player.

## Existing maps and driving

- Baycrest is a separate, San Francisco-inspired city selected from the title screen. Long grades lead into flatter intersections; stepped, textured row houses, bay windows, cable rails, waterfront and a red suspension bridge establish its character. Port Meridian remains selectable, with separate records and remembered city choice. The bridge and cable car are scenery, not drivable routes or vehicles.
- Six new districts: Painted Steps, Telegraph Terrace, Cable Yard, Ferry Market, Fort Point and Laurel Park. Telegraph Terrace has a climbable twelve-step approach to a 1.8 m platform. Landmark entrances and mission stops have matching collision clearance. Hill crests can hide the player from police sight.
- All modes begin in ordinary cars. Flat-ground simulation measures the Regent sedan at about 11.9 seconds to 60 mph, the compact at 14.1 and the wagon at 15.5. Acceleration tapers with speed; the starter still pulls away on steep grades. Hills, damage and handling alter actual results.
- Two rare, original performance cars are parked off the main streets in Baycrest: the low Vela GT (about 6.0 seconds to 60 mph) and long-hood Braddock V8 (about 7.1). They are excluded from normal traffic, unmarked until approached and retain damage after entering or leaving them.
- Baycrest gets its own ferry pickup, hill-country escape and ridge checkpoint jobs, with time limits suited to the slower starting cars.

## Retained systems

- Soft chase follows the vehicle while holding pedals. Deliberate looking temporarily overrides follow; walking also gets gentle follow with a stable movement direction. Exiting/entering recenters the camera, and on-foot orbits retract before nearby cars.
- Quieter HUD with a small local map, compact speed/condition display and a contextual entry/exit button. Pause contains recovery, controls and settings. Full-map landmark buttons set a destination.
- 113 occupied traffic vehicles on each grid-based map and 96 on the smaller Boston street network, across seven common silhouettes: sedan, coupe, wagon, hatchback, pickup, delivery van and taxi. Vehicle sizes, weights and performance differ. Traffic can make curved turns at junctions; distant cars use lightweight geometry.
- Safari gesture protection uses non-passive native touch handlers on Pointer Event game surfaces during play, in addition to CSS touch-action. Quick pedal taps produce 120 ms pulses; swipes cannot trigger them. Menu/settings scrolling and pinch zoom remain available outside play. Physical iPhone Safari verification is still required.
- Police track observed positions, search the last sighting, add backup during prolonged pursuit and return to patrol after escape. The second cruiser can lead a visible moving car. Full-body collisions damage cruisers; engine-disabled units cannot chase, spot or arrest. Siren volume follows distance. This remains an arcade pursuit system: foot officers patrol separately; cruisers do not yet deploy officers or build roadblocks.
- Up to ten persistent localized dents distinguish corner strikes from centered impacts. Severe side damage can bend wheels, squash their lower profile and remove sedan-family mirrors. Loose bumper ends move with driving. Body and mechanical damage remain separate.

- Floating left-thumb stick and free right-thumb yaw/pitch camera. Outer stick runs on foot. A swipe that starts on a pedal or interaction button remains a look gesture. Pedals engage after a 140 ms stationary hold and can then be held while looking. Tap-release interaction, per-pointer capture, and clearing on pause/blur/rotation prevent stuck or accidental input. Desktop drag and keyboard camera alternatives remain available.
- Occupied traffic cars can be taken at a side door while slow/stopped. A 1.1 second door interaction can be cancelled by moving. The driver leaves and begins a delayed report. Vehicle body/mechanical damage persists across ownership changes. Cars brake for traffic signals, people, and vehicles ahead.
- Seven distinct original landmarks: Clock Square, Union Depot, Iron Quay, Palm Court Motel, Mercer Gardens, Bayline Service and Foundry Row. The new forecourt and market alley have matching collision openings. A real diagonal Meridian Avenue cuts two blocks, with matching pavement openings and collision clearance. The full city map labels landmarks and marks the player, police, empty cars and job destination.
- Shared terrain heights drive the road mesh, wheels, shoes and camera. Two broad hills affect acceleration and walking speed. Three striped speed humps move damped suspension; crests can briefly unload the tyres. Forty-four pedestrians use sidewalk and crossing routes, move away from fast cars, and stumble or fall after a vehicle impact. The stationary on-foot player can also be hit, knocked back and briefly stunned before recovering. Swept car contact catches crossings between simulation positions; raised floors are excluded.
- Clock Square has twelve shallow steps and a 1.8 m terrace. Walking can climb the steps, cannot enter through the tall terrace side, and falls back to the ground when leaving an edge. Cars collide with the stair/terrace solids. Baycrest adds the similar Telegraph Terrace stair route; interiors and a citywide network of elevated paths are not implemented.
- Car contacts use oriented footprints and bilateral separation, momentum transfer and oblique yaw response. Traffic and parked vehicles can be pushed and damaged, including AI-to-AI contacts. This remains an arcade rigid-body approximation, not a full vehicle simulator.
- More severe directional crumpling, folded bonnet/boot, deep side dents, exposed metal, sagging bumpers, broken lights, cracked glass and smoke. Body condition is separate from engine/alignment condition: a battered car can still run. Hard front hits can make an engine sputter or stall; coupes are more vulnerable than wagons. Side impacts can pull the steering. Missions fail for a disabled engine, not cosmetic damage alone. Free-drive Recover repairs the current car.

## Controls

Desktop: WASD/arrows drive or walk; drag the view to look; Shift runs; Space handbrake; E exit/enter/take car; H horn; C recenter; V near/wide chase; J/L and I/K rotate camera; M map; R recover; Esc pause. Stop before exiting. Brake reverses after stopping.

Phone: lower-left floating stick steers or walks, outer edge runs. Tap GAS / BRAKE / DRIFT for a short pulse, or hold while driving. Drag the right side to look. Tap GET OUT, ENTER CAR or TAKE CAR as appropriate. Tap the speed readout to sound the horn. The circular arrow recenters. Tap the minimap for the street guide and destinations. Pause saves sensitivity, inverted vertical look, compact/roomy thumb spacing, wide camera and audio options. Both portrait and landscape are supported.

Mission checkpoints require a vehicle. Police pursue on foot too. Stay unseen eight seconds to lose them. Recover costs eight seconds in timed jobs. Focus loss pauses and clears input. Settings/records use localStorage with safe fallback. Audio unlocks on a user gesture.

## Multiplayer source and deployment

`dist/multiplayer-world.js` owns the shared simulation and per-player contexts. `dist/network-state.js` describes snapshots. `dist/multiplayer-client.js` handles input prediction, reconciliation, interpolation and reconnect. `server/rooms.mjs` owns room lifetimes and WebSocket validation. `server/server.mjs` serves the allowlisted game files, `/health`, and `/multiplayer`.

Run locally with `npm run dev -- --port 4173` and open the same address in two tabs or devices on your LAN. The public Render service remains on its existing free plan with its existing build/start commands. No additional service, database or paid plan was created. For reproducible deployment without an install step, `server/vendor/ws` contains the unmodified npm **ws 8.22.1** distribution and its MIT license. Update that vendored dependency deliberately when upstream security fixes are released.

The Sites mirror still serves static assets; its multiplayer client connects to the public Render server, which allows that exact mirror origin. Private room codes are invitations, not account access controls. A room disappears when all seats expire or the process restarts.

Multiplayer tests cover ownership races, traffic reservations, shared damage/explosions, player/person contact, isolated wanted levels, late-join destruction, returning entity definitions, finite prediction, input validation, stale-input braking, seat expiry and token reconnection. Real WebSocket tests exercise two clients, third-player rejection, shared damage, reconnect, replacement players, origin checks and oversized frame rejection. Browser checks cover desktop rooms plus 390×844 and 844×390 layouts. The cloud browser uses the software renderer; this does not certify a physical iPhone.
