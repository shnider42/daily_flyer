THE QUICK HUDDLE

Make the extra pass.

A familiar right-thumb diamond. Y sits above, X left, B right, and A below. A passes on offense and switches to the nearest player on defense. B shoots on offense and steals on defense. Y sends a leading bounce pass or an alley-oop setup on offense and blocks on defense. Hold X to sprint. The letters label the touch controls; keyboard alternatives are below.

Your team has the ball

Move: WASD / arrows or left stick. Your teammates follow the play you call.

Shoot: hold Space / SHOOT and release as the marker reaches green. Close to the rim, your player uses a layup or dunk. Open shots, strong ratings, fresh legs, and good timing help.

Pass: J / PASS finds a teammate in the direction you steer. Tap a player or their position button for a direct pass. You take control of the receiver.

Thru pass: aim with the left stick or movement keys and tap THRU / L to send a bounce pass ahead of a teammate. Aim toward the hoop from close enough with a teammate near the rim and THRU changes to LOB: tap it to set up an alley-oop. The receiver runs to the catch and tries the finish; defenders can break it up.

Screens: call High pick & roll in the playbook to bring a big up to screen, then roll toward the rim. Use 1–4 or the playbook to change the whole offense.

Defend or recover a loose ball

Switch: J / SWITCH selects your player closest to the ball. Q / Tab also switches; tap a defender for a specific selection.

Steal: tap Space / STEAL to swipe immediately when close to the ballhandler. E also steals. Reaching from a bad position can cause a foul.

Block: R / BLOCK contests a nearby shot. Stay between the ball and basket; hold C / Control to contain.

Sprint: hold Shift, K, or SPRINT. It drains energy. P / Escape pauses; T calls a timeout.

After winning the ball: move and pass immediately. Shooting has a half-second settling period; release the steal button and press again to start a shot. A held press never turns into a shot when possession changes, including rebounds. Presses during the settling period are ignored, so nothing fires later by itself.

Game pace: the default is 80% speed. Adjust it from 60–100% before a game or from Pause. Players, passes, shots, clocks, and the shot meter slow together. Sprint still gives you a burst, and moving without the ball stays faster than dribbling.

College rules, arcade pace

Five players a side. Two halves, a 30-second shot clock, 20 after an offensive rebound off the rim, ten seconds to cross half court, and two- or three-point baskets. Five personal fouls disqualify a player. The seventh team foul starts one-and-one; the tenth starts two free throws. Team fouls reset at halftime and carry into overtime. Free throws use the same shooting meter.

Quick games shorten the halves. Overtime is one minute, or five minutes with regulation-length halves. Automatic dribbling and inbounds keep the game moving. Foul detection is simplified; traveling, three-second violations, offensive fouls, goaltending, and administrative NCAA rules are not modeled. This is a solo arcade game, with no online multiplayer.

Guidance and difficulty are separate. Simple adds passing labels and coaching hints; Moderate keeps essential prompts; Expert clears the coaching text. Opponent difficulty changes CPU behavior.

Campus Cup: eight programs, three rounds, one champion. Other matchups are simulated. Completed-round progress saves locally; an unfinished game starts again if you reload.

Programs and their colors provide the setting. All players and ratings are original game content, not current rosters or official statistics. Rules reference: NCAA men's basketball.
