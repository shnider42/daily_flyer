# FARWAKE — The Quiet Earth

**Version 0.1.0 · DailyFlyer · Single-player browser game**

You begin beside Kettle Station in **Cinder**, a worn Sparrow-class hauler, with 180 credits and no fold drive. Earth has been silent for generations. Local jobs, old transmissions, and competing accounts of the evacuation gradually lead beyond your home system.

Farwake combines real-time ship flight with exploration on foot. The game is a static HTML/CSS/JavaScript application using Canvas 2D and browser-native ES modules. It has **no backend, accounts, multiplayer, or runtime AI service**.

## What is in this release

| Content | Current scope |
| --- | --- |
| Galaxy | **300 systems:** 12 authored hubs and 288 procedural systems |
| Campaign | Eight linked chapters involving both gameplay modes |
| Factions | Five, with persistent reputation and an authored alliance decision |
| Encounters | 25 authored choice events, including conditional follow-ups |
| Ship equipment | 16 upgrades, including three fold-drive tiers |
| Lore | 14 collectible records, plus location descriptions and NPC conversations |
| Local activities | Contract work, relay repairs, freight carrying, field samples, trade, and recruitment |

The procedural systems supply seeded places, encounters, resources, traffic, and threats around reusable surface layouts. They are not 288 separate handcrafted campaigns. The main story and encounter text are authored in [dist/content.mjs](dist/content.mjs).

### How the dynamic story works

Encounter eligibility depends on mode, location, resources, previous choices, and campaign flags. Eligible random encounters are selected using the game's saved pseudorandom state. Each option has explicit, deterministic costs and effects: credits, cargo, fuel, hull, reputation, lore, and persistent flags. Those flags can unlock later responses or encounters.

Dialogue and outcomes are not generated by a language model during play. The story is a finite set of authored branches whose availability changes with the voyage. The galaxy remains available for exploration after the campaign.

## Playing

### Ship mode

Steer Cinder through a local system, fire at hostile ships, mine nearby asteroids, investigate signals, collect salvage, and hail passing traffic. The mining beam selects a nearby rock in range. Shields recharge after a period without damage.

Approach a station, planet, or moon until the context button offers docking or landing. Use that prompt to enter person mode. Some derelict signals also offer boarding. You can enter Cinder's cabin from the ship controls and return through its helm.

Fold travel is selected on the starmap and requires a drive with sufficient range and enough fuel. Local flight does not consume jump fuel. Ports provide repairs, refueling, trading, and emergency assistance; ship destruction uses a recovery penalty rather than deleting the voyage.

### Person mode

Walk through stations, settlements, your cabin, and boardable derelicts. Approach an NPC or workstation and use **Talk / Use**. Available activities include conversations, contract collection, carrying a crate to a loading pad, collecting field samples, and calibrating maintenance relays with a small conductor puzzle.

Contract payment is collected at the stated destination's job board after the requirements are met. A maintenance contract requires three distinct repairs. Return to a landing shuttle, airlock, or helm to resume ship mode.

Person mode currently focuses on exploration, work, and decisions. The implemented real-time weapon combat is in ship mode.

### A useful opening route

1. Dock at Kettle Station and meet Iona at the shipyard.
2. Take a maintenance shift from the contract exchange. Repair the three marked relays and return for payment.
3. Launch, mine six ore, and investigate the old evacuation beacon. The journal and local map can track objectives.
4. Earn and fit Vector thrusters and an Echo scanner, then buy Fold Drive I. Its calibration also requires the local beacon chapter.
5. Follow the next campaign destination or take more local work before leaving.

The first two prerequisite upgrades and the drive cost **500 credits and 12 ore combined**: 85/2 for the thrusters, 95/2 for the scanner, and 320/8 for the drive. Fold-drive ranges are 5, 9, and 15 light-years. Optional equipment improves cargo capacity, hull, shields, weapons, mining, fuel capacity, and handling.

The campaign requires actions and decisions, not just arrival at a destination. Later objectives remain in the journal without revealing their outcomes here.

## Controls

### Keyboard

| Input | Action |
| --- | --- |
| **WASD** or **arrow keys** | Steer in ship mode; walk in person mode |
| **E** | Context action: dock, land, talk, use, collect, or investigate |
| **Space** | Hold to fire in ship mode; interact on foot |
| **R** | Hold to mine in ship mode; open the mission log on foot |
| **Shift** | Boost in ship mode; run on foot |
| **X** | Brake the ship |
| **F** | Scan a nearby signal |
| **M** | Open the map; on foot it starts with the local view |
| **J** | Open the journal |
| **I** | Open ship and cargo information |
| **C** | Enter Cinder's cabin while in ship mode |
| **Esc** or **P** | Pause; close an open menu |

On the galaxy map, drag to pan, select a star to inspect it, and use the wheel or **+ / −** buttons to zoom. Search accepts system names. Selecting a destination does not jump immediately: use **Engage fold drive** when the range and fuel checks permit it.

### Touch

The movement stick and action buttons accept separate fingers. Hold the right-hand controls to **Fire**, **Mine**, or **Boost**. In person mode they become **Use**, **Log**, and **Run**. The context strip also handles docking and nearby interactions. Map, log, and ship buttons remain available below the playfield. The ship button also opens access to your cabin and crew manifest.

Pause settings can enlarge the touch controls and swap the joystick to the right side. Input is cleared when a menu opens, the layout changes, the page loses focus, or a touch is cancelled, preventing a held action from carrying across those transitions.

## Saves and restoration

The game uses **one local save slot per browser origin**. Progress is stored in `localStorage`, with automatic saves roughly every 12 seconds of active play, after many actions and menu transitions, and when the page is hidden or left. Menus pause the simulation.

Use **Continue voyage** to restore the current local save. Starting a fresh voyage replaces that slot after a confirmation. Different domains or ports have separate browser storage; there is no cloud synchronization.

To move a voyage between devices or keep a backup:

1. Open **Pause → Export / import save → Download save**. This downloads `farwake-voyage.json`.
2. On the destination browser, begin or continue a voyage and open the same save panel.
3. Open the exported JSON as text, paste its contents into the import box, and choose **Check and import save**. Import replaces the current local voyage.

The save includes the galaxy seed, pseudorandom state, ship and crew, resources, contracts, decisions, campaign progress, discoveries, and recorded changes to visited systems. Within the same game version, restoration rebuilds the same seeded galaxy and reapplies its saved changes. A seed by itself reproduces the base world; the full save is needed to restore the player's history.

Settings are stored separately and are not included in the voyage export. The current engine save format is version 3; the browser storage keys retain their existing names:

- Voyage: `dfe.farwake.save.v1`
- Preferences: `dfe.farwake.settings.v1`

The HUD reports **Save unavailable** if browser storage cannot be written. The export action is available from the save panel independently of that local-storage write.

## Settings and audio

Pause provides guidance selection (**Simple**, **Moderate**, or **Expert**), touch-control size from 80–120%, interface text size from 100–120%, flight assist, swapped thumb controls, sound effects, ambient music, and reduced motion.

Flight assist slows the ship when movement input is released; disabling it allows more drift. Expert guidance hides the detailed objective hint and route markers. Reduced motion starts from the browser's preference and can be changed in the game.

Audio is synthesized locally with Web Audio. It starts after a browser-accepted user gesture. The ambient score has its own switch, but the current sound-enable setting also acts as the audio master: sound must be enabled for the score to play.

## Running locally

`dist/` contains the editable source files and the complete served application. There is no bundler or build step, and no package installation is required to serve the game or run its tests.

From the project directory, use a local HTTP server:

```sh
python3 -m http.server 8080 --bind 127.0.0.1 --directory dist
```

Open [http://127.0.0.1:8080](http://127.0.0.1:8080) in a browser with JavaScript enabled. Serve the files over HTTP instead of opening `index.html` through a `file:` URL, so browser module loading works consistently.

For static hosting, publish the **contents of `dist/`** with `index.html` at the site root. Keep relative module paths intact and serve `.mjs` files with a JavaScript MIME type. No environment variables, API keys, database, or server application are required.

The engine exposes action methods such as `dock`, `travel`, `scan`, `buyUpgrade`, `triggerEncounter`, and `applyChoice`. Actions return structured results, while `drainEvents()` supplies notifications to the browser layer. `save()` returns a plain save object, `serialize()` returns JSON, and `Game.fromSave()` restores it.

When changing narrative content, keep IDs and flags stable where existing saves refer to them. Resource costs belong in both choice conditions and negative effects. Story interactions use authored encounters; keep their flags aligned with chapter conditions and engine gates. Re-run progression tests after changing prerequisites, rewards, or destinations.

## Tests and release checks

Run the automated suite from the project directory:

```sh
node --test tests/*.test.mjs
```

The equivalent package command is `npm test`. `npm run check` performs syntax checks on the three core application, engine, and surface modules.

**Verified on 2026-11-08 with Node v24.22.1: 39 tests passed, 0 failed, 0 skipped (19 engine, 16 surface, 4 input).** The suite covers galaxy determinism and connectivity, mode/proximity rules, contract accounting and abandonment/reacceptance, resources and upgrades, persistent world changes, crew and interior restoration, all eight campaign stages, encounter gates, projectile collisions, independent touch ownership, surface reachability, and movement collision.

These are automated rule and surface checks. For a release, also exercise the browser UI: start a voyage, dock and launch, complete a repair and contract, make an encounter choice, export and import a save, resize the layout, and try simultaneous touch movement/actions. Audio activation and touch behavior still need checks on the intended physical devices. A 414 × 896 iframe was used during development to check phone layout dimensions and scrolling; that is not proof of physical-device compatibility.

## Art and shared-code provenance

Farwake's pixel-style planets, ships, stations, characters, terrain, rooms, and portraits are original drawings produced by the code in this project. The application does not load an external sprite pack or recorded music library. Its effects and ambient notes are generated with Web Audio.

The two files under `dist/shared/` are reused DailyFlyer/DFE helpers. Farwake-specific input and sound behavior lives in `input.mjs` and `audio.mjs`; the shared helpers retain the existing project's multi-touch and device-audio handling.
