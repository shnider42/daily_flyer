Adapted from the original Sand and Stone FLACs. Six recorded takes per
material, ordered L1/R1/L2/R2/L3/R3 in fixed 0.5-second slots; mono 22,050 Hz

The uploader describes three single shots from an M249 machine gun recorded

Both banks adapt the same three recorded shots. The SAW bank uses the M249
source directly as its recorded basis. The carbine/rifle bank is a separate

Processed for CHALK from the publisher's HQ MP3 preview: isolate shots at
8.727667, 10.617063, and 11.824292 seconds, retaining 1.5 ms before each onset;
convert to mono at 44,100 Hz; high-pass at 55 Hz and low-pass at 11 kHz.
The rifle treatment adds upper-frequency emphasis above 1.5 kHz; the SAW
treatment reinforces the recorded 120–650 Hz body. Each uses its own body
envelope and decay shaping after 65 ms to reduce the original range tail.
Apply a 0.3 ms opening fade and 28 ms closing fade, remove DC offset, and
normalize each take to a peak of 0.86. The rifle takes last 0.42 seconds;
the SAW takes last 0.28 seconds. Each three-take bank uses 0.6-second slots
and is saved as mono 16-bit PCM WAV. Reproduction script:
tools/prepare-weapon-audio.py

Propagation and weapon playback design — 23 November 2026

CHALK uses a reference sound speed of 343 metres/second at its displayed
scale of 8 world units/metre. Reports and impacts travel from their separate
source positions and source timestamps; network elapsed time is subtracted
from the remaining travel time. This is a gameplay approximation, not a
reconstruction of atmospheric conditions during the historical battle.
Reference physics: NASA Glenn, Speed of Sound Interactive:
https://www1.grc.nasa.gov/beginners-guide-to-aeronautics/speed-of-sound-interactive/

Weapon playback introduced in version 1.11 uses the three-take rifle and SAW banks
credited above, alongside hostile-shot.mp3 for opposing weapons and the sniper.
Report length, filtering, level, and restrained pitch variation shape their
playback. The previous additional per-shot mechanical and synthetic body-noise
layers have been removed from recorded gunfire. If a recording cannot load,
a cached procedural report supplies a fallback; it is not a firearm recording.
The remaining weapon families do not each have a separately sourced recording.
All earlier recording and music assets remain in the project.

The near-pass crack is a short procedural bipolar/broadband transient. It is
triggered only by the simulation's actual collision-clipped near-pass event
for the listener and only at a projectile speed above the reference sound
speed. Its short lateral sound travel uses the closest-approach approximation.
Concrete chips and opened-wall debris tails are procedural; a heavier tail
requires a blast that actually breached geometry, not a cosmetic dust effect.

Room and street reflection design — 23 November 2026

The room and street stereo response kernels are procedural sound design made
for CHALK. Seeded noise, shaped decay, and discrete reflection transients produce
a short, dense room response and a later, more widely spaced urban street
response. These are not recorded or measured impulse responses of actual rooms,
streets, Mogadishu buildings, or locations from the historical battle.

The game reuses these response buffers and selects their sends using the
source and listener locations, room enclosure, walls, doors, and explosive
openings. This is a bounded geometry-based approximation, not a full sound-wave
simulation. The Room & street reflections control changes the reflected layer
without removing direct effects. Existing recordings and music assets remain.

Version 1.11 also adds at most two early facade reflections per eligible shot.
These reuse the report and follow paths from the source to an actual remaining
exterior wall section and then to the listener. Path length, surface position,
and obstruction checks determine delay, stereo placement, and filtering;
doors and breaches can remove a reflecting section. The extra reflections
apply to clear outdoor hearing. Indoor and obstructed hearing retain the
existing room and indirect mix. This supplements the shared later street
response within the existing 32-voice limit; it is a bounded gameplay model,
not measured acoustics or a full sound-wave simulation.

Version 1.12 adds a separate procedural 2.8-second diffuse outdoor response.
It uses its own seeded noise and shaped decay, with no additional recording.
Only unobstructed gunfire with both source and listener outdoors uses this
layer; city density sets its strength. The previous room and indirect street
response kernels are unchanged. Version 1.13 corrects its original doorway
cutoff: the existing outdoor tail and scheduled returns keep decaying while
the listener transfer smoothly attenuates and low-pass filters that energy.
New indoor reports still do not feed the exterior response. Deliberate pause,
mute and listener changes flush old history.

A sufficiently large, unobstructed distant facade can add one later report
return, 0.18-0.55 seconds after the direct arrival, with a lower level and
less high-frequency content. Early plus late returns total at most three
per shot. A global 0.24-second spacing and two-voice late-echo cap prevent
automatic bursts from reserving the voice budget for pending echoes. Doors,
breaches and intervening solids still govern the actual path. No randomly
placed or prerecorded environmental echo is substituted for this geometry.
